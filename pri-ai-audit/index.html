Takeaway

What this portfolio demonstrates about operationalizing AI in a scientific survey

Across 23 repositories, real AI-in-production work clusters in a small, identifiable set: ras-agent (agentic hydraulic modeling orchestration with an explicit human-signoff gate), champ-contacts-review (the strongest governance example — AI proposes with cited sources, a human commits to the system of record, and the two are structurally separated), and champ-pm (production payroll/grant tooling with a narrow, dry-run-gated AI feature). The rest split into experimental physics/ML pipelines (floodstack, risk-agent, il-triton, ras-fis-profile) that are honestly self-labeled as prototypes, a personal consulting/ministry product line (pastordave-*, baseflood-*) that is commercially mature but organizationally separate from CHAMP's scientific mission, and several trivial or abandoned repos excluded from this presentation.

The pattern worth highlighting to the committee is not volume of AI usage but discipline in scoping it: every production-grade tool keeps AI confined to proposal/summarization/orchestration roles while routing anything consequential (database writes, payroll syncs, model promotion) through an explicit, logged human gate. No repo in this portfolio gives an AI system unsupervised write access to authoritative data. That constraint, more than any single tool, is the transferable institutional lesson.
